A RACI matrix assigns responsibility for every major deliverable across five roles. This one describes how the program would be governed in an organizational deployment, not how the portfolio build actually ran, which collapsed all five roles into one person. It is a sample of what a RACI matrix could look like. The roles and assignments could change significantly with how a given organization is structured.

Select a role header to see its definition and totals; filter by phase to narrow the grid. Every cell is labeled R, A, C, or I.

## What the distribution shows

The L&D Manager, as program owner, carries most of the accountability and approves deliverables before they advance. The Executive Sponsor is Accountable only at the strategic decision points: the needs-analysis and design-review approvals, the resource budget, and the Level-4 ROI model. The sponsor engages at phase gates without being drawn into operational design. The Subject-Matter Expert's involvement is front-loaded into the first three phases. In an AI-literacy program the subject matter changes fast, so SME time is best spent during content development and periodic refresh, not sustained through implementation and deployment.

## Solo build versus team execution

In the actual portfolio build, all five roles collapsed into the solo practitioner. The matrix makes explicit what that collapse obscures. A production deployment needs domain expertise from the SME, technical implementation from the FS, organizational authority from the ES, design methodology from the ID, and program governance from the LM. One person rarely combines all five.
